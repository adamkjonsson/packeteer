# Plan — v0.15.0: the real-traffic release

*Written 2026-09-13.*

*Status: in progress on `v_0.15.0`.*

0.14.0 was shaped by what happened when 0.12.0 and 0.13.0 were **used**. This
one is shaped by what happened when packeteer was pointed at traffic it did
not write. Every issue in it was either filed for the real-capture corpus or
found while collecting for it.

The corpus exists because of #89's observation, which `MANIFEST.md` still
quotes: *every capture CI can see is one packeteer generated itself*, and
across the eleven synthetic ones **0 of 1 314 TCP packets carry a TCP
option**. #87 existed because of that. #127 is the standing record of what the
corpus still cannot see, and until this release every capture in it came from
one macOS laptop.

A Linux machine closes some of that by existing. It also turned out to be an
instrument: collecting five captures on it found two bugs, and neither is in
the capture path.

**The two bugs.** #151 is `sanitise` leaving the inner addresses and MACs of a
VXLAN, Geneve or GTP-U tunnel untouched — silently, reporting success, in a
function whose entire job is redaction. #152 is `parse` → `build` failing to
reproduce a raw-IP capture, which gains an Ethernet header and padding on the
way back out. Both are the #101/#117/#122/#123/#125 shape: a supported input,
a green suite, and a wrong answer nothing asks about. Both are also
**prerequisites**: no tunnel capture can be committed while #151 stands, and
no raw-IP capture can join a corpus whose sweep asserts a byte-identical
rebuild while #152 does.

**The captures.** #149 asks for one specific file — a lossy TCP session where
both ends negotiated the Timestamps option — and names the three things a
consumer would assert against it. #127 is the umbrella. Both are collected and
verified already; this release commits them and writes the assertions.

**The generator.** #150 is the other half of #149. #90 made every resend carry
a fresh clock, which is what tells a retransmission from a duplicate — and
left packeteer unable to generate the duplicate. #149's capture is what a real
one looks like; #150 makes one.

## The order, and why

```
#151 → #152 → #149 → #127 → #150 → docs sweep → release
```

- **#151 first.** It is a privacy bug in a public repository's tooling, and it
  is the gate on #127: adding a VXLAN capture before it lands would commit
  real inner addresses with every existing check green. It is also small — the
  fix is three keys — and the interesting half is the sweep that should have
  caught it.
- **#152 second.** The other gate, and the smaller one. `ipip_raw.pcap` is
  collected and cannot join the corpus until `parse` → `build` reproduces it,
  because `TestRoundTrip` sweeps every file in the directory.
- **#149 third.** Additive, and depends on neither fix — its two captures are
  plain Ethernet. It goes after the bugs because it establishes the corpus
  test vocabulary (`_packets`, per-file assertions) that #127 then extends,
  and because a `.dev0` build carrying the capture is what
  python-zipline-wire#17 is waiting on.
- **#127 fourth.** It is the collection work, and it needs both fixes landed.
- **#150 last, and largest by a distance.** It is the only issue that changes
  generated bytes, so every test asserting on a seeded capture is touched in
  one window rather than several — 0.14.0 made the same argument for #131 and
  #90. It also wants #149 landed first: the real duplicate capture is the
  reference for what the synthetic one should look like, and "same TSval, same
  checksum, adjacent timestamp" is easier to assert against a file than to
  argue from the RFC. **If the milestone has to ship before it is finished,
  #150 is what moves.** Nothing ahead of it depends on it.

Sizing, honestly: #151 and #152 are each half a day including the tests that
should have caught them; #149 is a day, mostly writing assertions that are
true rather than plausible; #127 is two to three days, most of it collecting
and verifying rather than coding; #150 is the week. The sweep is a day.

# Part 1 — #151, a tunnel the redactor does not enter

## The bug, precisely

`_sanitise_packet` recurses into a nested tunnel, over a list that is missing
three of the six keys that nest one:

```python
for tunnel_key in ("ipip", "gre", "etherip", "pseudowire", "ah"):
```

`parse` nests under `vxlan`, `geneve` and `gtpu` exactly as it does under the
five above — `config["vxlan"] = inner` in `to_config.py`, and the same at
`_apply_geneve` and `_apply_gtpu`. For those three the inner packet is never
redacted at all.

Measured, outer `203.0.113.9 → 198.51.100.7`, inner `8.8.8.8 → 1.1.1.1`:

```
vxlan   inner IPs: 8.8.8.8,1.1.1.1   inner MACs: de:ad:be:ef:00:01,…   warnings: 0
geneve  inner IPs: 8.8.8.8,1.1.1.1   inner MACs: de:ad:be:ef:00:01,…   warnings: 0
gtpu    inner IPs: 8.8.8.8,1.1.1.1   inner MACs: -                     warnings: 0
gre     inner IPs: -                 inner MACs: -                     warnings: 0
```

Every outer field is redacted correctly. MPLS and PPPoE are unaffected: they
do not nest, so the inner IP lands in the top-level `network` section and is
redacted there.

It is silent because all three keys are already in `_STRUCTURAL_KEYS`, so
`_warn_undecoded` sees a packet that *was* decoded and says nothing.

## The fix, and the part that matters

The fix is the three keys. The interesting question is why nothing caught it.

`TestNothingIdentifyingSurvived.test_no_globally_routable_addresses` in
`test_real_corpus.py` is the sweep whose whole job is this, and it reads
`pkt.ip` — which on a tunnelled packet is the **outer** header. Given a VXLAN
packet with outer `192.0.2.1 → 192.0.2.2` and inner `8.8.8.8`, it sees the
outer pair, finds nothing global, and passes. Verified.

So two changes, and the second is the one worth having:

1. Add `vxlan`, `geneve`, `gtpu` to the recursion.
2. **Make the sweep walk inner packets.** It should follow `tunneled` to
   whatever depth the parse produced, so a leak of this shape fails CI rather
   than waiting to be noticed by hand while collecting captures.

### <a name="q1"></a>Q1 — one list or two

`_STRUCTURAL_KEYS` and the tunnel-recursion tuple are two hand-maintained
lists of overlapping membership, and this bug is exactly what that costs: all
three keys were added to the first and forgotten in the second.

*Recommendation: derive the recursion list from a single named constant —
`_NESTING_TUNNEL_KEYS` — and keep `_STRUCTURAL_KEYS` a superset of it by
construction.* The two are not the same set (`esp`, `mpls` and `pppoe` are
structural but do not nest), so they cannot simply be one name; but the
nesting set should exist once and be referenced, not repeated.

## Changelog

Under `Fixed`. It is a redaction failure in a tool whose output people commit,
so it says plainly which encapsulations were affected and that a file produced
by an earlier version should be regenerated.

## Tests

- Per-encapsulation: for each of the six nesting keys, an inner address and an
  inner MAC, asserted absent from the sanitised spec. The three that leaked
  and the three that did not, so the test states the whole rule.
- The corpus sweep extended to `tunneled`, with a fixture that would have
  failed before it.

### What #151 corrected

**Q1 settled as recommended.** `_NESTING_TUNNEL_KEYS` is the single record of
which encapsulations nest a packet; `_STRUCTURAL_KEYS` is built from it as a
superset, and `_sanitise_packet` iterates the constant rather than a literal.
The two lists can no longer disagree because there is now one.

**The test that matters is not the per-encapsulation one.** Asserting that
VXLAN, Geneve and GTP-U now redact their inner packet is a regression test: it
fails for exactly the bug that was fixed and would not have caught it in
advance. The one that would have is
`test_every_parser_nesting_key_is_listed`, which reads `to_config.py` for the
`config["<key>"] = inner` pattern and asserts every key it finds is recursed
into. That derives the expectation from the parser — the component that
actually decides what nests — rather than from a second copy of the list. It
finds all eight keys today, and would fail the day a tenth encapsulation is
added to the parser and nowhere else.

**Verified against the pre-fix code**: restoring the old five-key list fails
11 assertions across both classes. A regression test that has never been seen
to fail is a guess.

**The corpus sweep's blind spot was the more serious half.** The leak could
have been committed with every check green, because
`test_no_globally_routable_addresses` read `ParsedPacket.ip` — the *outer*
header, which on a tunnelled packet holds the capture point's own addresses
rather than the traffic being carried. `_every_address` now walks `tunneled`
to any depth and reports which layer a leak came from.

# Part 2 — #152, a link layer that is not there

## The bug

`LINKTYPE_RAW` (101) is in `SUPPORTED_LINK_TYPES`, parses without warning, and
reaches the IP layer. `parse` → `build` returns something 31 bytes longer:

```
original: 4500001d000040004011b6c5c0000201c0000209303900090009d38e78
rebuilt : 0000000000020000000000010800 4500001d…d38e78 0000…0000
          └─ 14 bytes of Ethernet ─┘                  └ 17 of padding ┘
```

`_apply_spec_to_builder` in `__main__.py` chooses the link layer as a chain —
`loopback`, `elif sll`, `elif sll2`, `else ethernet` — with no branch for a
link type that has **no** link-layer header, so a raw-IP packet falls into the
`else` and is then padded to the 60-byte Ethernet minimum.

`PacketBuilder` is not at fault: `PacketBuilder().ip(...).udp(...)` already
emits a bare IP frame, which is what `_frame_for(LINKTYPE_RAW)` in
`test_link_type_support.py` relies on.

## Why nothing caught it

Nothing round-trips a raw-IP capture. `test_link_type_support.py` covers
`LINKTYPE_RAW` for *parsing* and never rebuilds it; `test_real_corpus.py`'s
`TestRoundTrip` is the sweep that would, and every capture in the corpus is
Ethernet, `DLT_NULL` or Linux cooked.

## The fix

A packet whose spec carries none of `ethernet`, `sll`, `sll2`, `loopback` or
`arp`, but does carry `network`, is a raw-IP packet and is built with no link
layer.

### <a name="q2"></a>Q2 — infer from the spec, or read `metadata.link_type`

The spec's own shape already says this, and `_apply_spec_to_builder` works per
packet without the file's metadata in hand. Reading `metadata.link_type` would
mean threading it through, and would break the recursive call for an EtherIP
inner frame, which has no metadata of its own.

*Recommendation: infer from the spec's shape, and make the raw case explicit
rather than a fall-through — the `else` becomes `elif` on the link-layer keys,
with the bare-IP case named.*

## Tests

- **A round-trip case for every member of `SUPPORTED_LINK_TYPES`**, so the
  sweep covers the set rather than the corpus's accidental sample of it. This
  is the test whose absence is the actual bug.
- `ipip_raw.pcap` joins the corpus in Part 4, which makes it a standing case.

# Part 3 — #149, a session that loses something

## What the captures are

Both collected 2026-09-13 on Linux 6.6.87.2 (WSL2), in three throwaway network
namespaces — client, router, server — with `tc netem` on the router. Both have
been through `packeteer sanitise`; the unsanitised originals are outside the
repository.

| File | Packets | Bytes | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tcp_lossy_ts.pcap` | 234 | 93 813 | 10% loss on the router's client-facing leg, 20 ms each way, captured **at the sender** |
| `tcp_dup_ts.pcap` | 154 | 50 653 | 15% duplication, captured **on the router** |

Two details of the capture method matter enough to record, because both were
got wrong on the first attempt and both produce a file that looks fine:

- **The router is not decoration.** An egress `netem` drops a packet at
  enqueue, which is *before* AF_PACKET's transmit tap, so a capture taken on
  the dropping device never holds the segment that went missing. #149's first
  assertion compares a resend against its original, so the loss has to happen
  downstream of tcpdump.
- **`gso_max_segs 1` and a 20 ms delay.** The first attempt captured
  "segments" of up to 7 000 bytes, because the stack hands one large skb to
  the device and defers segmentation; and it ran an entire 32 KiB transfer
  inside a single 1 ms timestamp tick, so every TSval in the file was
  identical and a fast retransmit carried the *same* TSval as its original.
  A veth pair is too fast for the question #149 asks.

## What is true of them

Measured on the sanitised files, which is what gets committed:

| #149's assertion | Result |
|---|---|
| 1. A resend carries a later TSval than the original | **14 / 14** |
| 2. Dup ACKs echo the last in-order segment, not the out-of-order one | **28 observable, 0 violations** |
| 3. The ACK answering the retransmission echoes its TSval | **10 / 10** |
| Optional: a genuine duplicate | **10**, identical seq *and* TSval |

Assertion 2 needs its number explained, and the explanation belongs in the
manifest row rather than in a comment. Of 39 duplicate ACKs, 28 echo a TSval
strictly older than the out-of-order segment that triggered them — the rule
visibly holding. The other 10 echo a TSval equal to the trigger's, because the
in-order segment and the out-of-order one were sent inside the same 1 ms tick
and *share* a TSval. Those are indistinguishable, not counterexamples: in each
case some in-order segment carries that same TSval. There are **no** cases
where the echo could only have come from the out-of-order segment.

That ceiling is a property of the option's granularity, not of this capture. A
future capture cannot do better, which is why the manifest should say so.

### <a name="q3"></a>Q3 — one file or two

#149 allows the duplicate to be "a second file rather than the same one". They
are two files here because they need opposite capture points: the lossy one
must be captured at the sender to hold the original of a dropped segment, and
the duplicate one must be captured where the duplication happens or later.

*Recommendation: keep them separate, and say in each row why the capture point
is where it is.* A capture whose vantage is unexplained is one nobody can
extend later.

## Corpus mechanics

- `.gitignore` gains `!testcases/real/tcp_lossy_ts.pcap` and
  `!testcases/real/tcp_dup_ts.pcap`. Naming each file is what stops an
  unsanitised one being committed by being dropped in the directory.
- `MANIFEST.md` gains a row each, saying what the file is *for* — the three
  assertions, and the same-tick ceiling on the second.
- Both are classic `.pcap` in the nanosecond format, which is what
  `tcpdump -w --nano` writes and what `sanitise --pcap` preserves.

## Tests

In `TestRealTrafficCoversWhatSyntheticCannot`, one test per assertion, each
named for the property rather than the file. They must assert what is true:
assertion 2's test asserts *no violations*, not a count of 39, or it will fail
the next time the traffic is regenerated.

# Part 4 — #127, the corpus reaches an encapsulation

## Where it stands

`MANIFEST.md`'s gap list is the specification. Two of its entries are closed by
captures already collected and verified:

- **Linux cooked capture.** `sll_any.pcap` (link type 113, 36 packets) and
  `sll2_any.pcap` (276, 40 packets), both from `tcpdump -i any`; the `any`
  device offers both encodings and `-y` picks v1. Both round-trip.
- **A *captured* nanosecond timestamp.** Every capture in this release is
  `nsecpcap` with genuinely non-zero sub-microsecond digits — 80/80 on the
  probe. `udp_frag_nano.pcap` only ever had the format, having been converted
  with `editcap` from a microsecond capture.

The entry the collecting *added* is `ipip_raw.pcap` (link type 101, 12
packets): a capture taken directly on an `ipip` device, which libpcap reports
as `DLT_RAW` because a tunnel device has no link-layer header it can name. It
is the capture that found #152, and it joins the corpus once #152 lands.

## What this release collects

The largest listed gap is *"VLAN, MPLS, and any tunnel"* — nine modules with
no real traffic. This kernel reaches most of them; the harness from Part 3
already builds the topologies.

| Encapsulation | Available here | Note |
|---|---|---|
| VXLAN | yes | built into the kernel (`CONFIG_VXLAN=y`) |
| Geneve, GRE, IP-in-IP, VLAN | yes | modules present and loadable |
| SCTP | yes | `sctp.ko` present; a real association, not a synthesised one |
| IPv6 extension headers | yes | a fragmented IPv6 datagram closes the other half of #68's case |
| PPPoE | probably | `pppoe.ko` present, needs a server to talk to |
| **MPLS** | **no** | `mpls_router` and `mpls_iptunnel` are not built for this kernel |
| **An FCS** | **no** | hardware strips it; a virtual NIC never has one |

### <a name="q4"></a>Q4 — does #127 close in 0.15.0

No. MPLS and an FCS are both out of reach on this machine, and an FCS is out
of reach on any virtual interface. GTP-U has no Linux tunnel driver either —
it would have to be forged, which is what the corpus exists to avoid.

*Recommendation: #127 stays open with its gap list reduced to what it still
cannot see, and the manifest says why each remaining entry is hard rather than
merely undone.* Closing an umbrella issue because most of it is done is how a
gap list stops being trustworthy.

## The size budget

`test_the_corpus_stays_small` caps the directory at 512 KiB. Today it is
76 496 bytes; Part 3's two files take it to 221 KiB, and Part 4's additions
should land it near 300 KiB.

`tcp_lossy_ts.pcap` is 93 KiB of that, the largest single file by a distance.
`editcap` is available, so if the budget gets tight the answer is to trim it to
a window holding two complete loss-and-recovery cycles — but not before the
assertions are written, since trimming changes every count in them.

## Tests and manifest

Each new capture gets a `.gitignore` entry, a manifest row saying what it is
*for*, and at least one assertion naming the decoder it exercises. A capture
nothing asserts anything about is a file, not a test — and Part 1's extended
leak sweep now walks the inner packets of every one of them, which is the
check that makes committing a tunnel capture safe at all.

# Part 5 — #150, the copy the generator cannot make

## What is missing

#90 made every resend carry the sender's clock at resend time. That is right,
and it is what lets an analyser tell a retransmission from a duplicate. The
other half has no generator: a **duplicate** is one transmission seen twice by
the *capture point* — a SPAN port, a two-interface capture, a veth pair. The
sender did nothing; bytes, sequence numbers, checksum and TSval are identical.

`retransmission_probability` is the sender's act and now carries a new clock,
so no configuration of `generate_tcp_stream` produces a same-TSval repeat.
Part 3's `tcp_dup_ts.pcap` is what one looks like, with 10 of them.

## The shape

A capture-point impairment beside the wire ones in `impairments.py`:

- `duplicate_probability: float = 0.0` on `ImpairmentConfig`, and mirrored on
  `TCPStreamConfig` the way the other rates are.
- Every packet is a candidate, **data or ACK** — a mirror doubles everything,
  which is what distinguishes this from every existing pass, all of which run
  over `data_idx`.
- The copy is `raw` **verbatim**: same TSval, same TSecr, same checksum. This
  is the one pass that must *not* go through `_restamped`, and the docstring
  should say so, because `_restamped` exists precisely because the other
  passes must.
- Its timestamp is the original's plus a small offset through `_alloc_usec`,
  so it stays unique and adjacent. A mirror delivers the copy microseconds
  later, not an RTO later.
- Applied **last**, after loss, retransmission, corruption and strays, so a
  retransmission can itself be duplicated and a corrupted copy is doubled as
  corrupted — what a mirror would see.
- Labelled `DUP[…]` via `_derive_label`, as `RETRANS` and `CORRUPT` are.

`any_post_pass` gains it, so a stream configured with only this impairment
still reaches the post pass.

### <a name="q5"></a>Q5 — `--stream` flag or config field only

#150 says *"not a `--stream` flag until someone asks; the config field is
enough for a consumer's test suite."*

*Recommendation: follow the issue.* But CLAUDE.md's rule is that everything in
the CLI must be reachable from the API, not that everything in the API must be
in the CLI — so this is consistent, and worth a line in the changelog saying
the field is API-only for now.

### <a name="q6"></a>Q6 — what the duplicate offset should be

A fixed offset is reproducible and trivially assertable; a jittered one looks
more like a real mirror. `tcp_dup_ts.pcap` can answer this rather than
taste — the real inter-copy gaps are measurable from it.

*Recommendation: decide from the capture, and record the measured distribution
in the plan when the answer is known.* This is the one question in the release
with real evidence available, and it would be a waste not to use it.

## Tests

- A seeded stream with `duplicate_probability=1.0`: every packet appears
  exactly twice, and each pair is byte-identical apart from its timestamp.
- The pairs' TSvals are **equal** — the property, asserted directly against a
  stream that negotiated timestamps.
- Ordering: a duplicate of a retransmission exists when both rates are set,
  which is what "applied last" buys.
- Determinism: the same seed gives the same capture; a zero rate draws no
  randomness and leaves a stream byte-identical to one generated without the
  field, which is the guarantee `ImpairmentConfig`'s docstring already makes.

## Changelog

Under `Added`. It changes generated bytes only when configured, so it is not a
breaking change — but the release notes should say that a stream with
impairments configured is unaffected unless the new field is set.

# Part 6 — the documentation sweep

Its own step, after the last issue and before any release preparation. Each
issue updates the pages it touches; the sweep reads the whole of `docs/` and
`README.md` against what the branch now does, which is a different job.

Where this release will have leaked:

- **`sanitise`'s documentation** claims to redact tunnels. After #151 that is
  true; before it, it was the claim that made the bug invisible. Whatever it
  says now should be checked against what it does.
- **Link-type documentation** gains a raw-IP round trip that did not work.
- **The corpus.** `MANIFEST.md` is documentation that CI enforces, and it will
  have been edited five times by five parts. It needs one read as a whole —
  particularly the gap list, which is the thing people cite.
- **Impairment documentation** gains a pass that is unlike the others in the
  one respect the module's own comments emphasise.
- **Transcripts and version strings**, per 0.13.0's sweep (`4624f84`), which
  found one two releases stale.
